---
term: Term premium
shortDef: The extra yield investors demand for holding longer-dated bonds, beyond what's implied by expected future short-term interest rates.
category: rates
---

If markets were perfectly efficient and risk-neutral, a 10-year yield would just equal the average of expected 1-year rates over the next decade. In practice, investors demand a small bonus — the *term premium* — for taking duration risk: the chance that inflation surprises higher, that fiscal supply overwhelms demand, or that they need their money before maturity.

Term premium can't be observed directly because expected future rates aren't observable. It has to be estimated from models like Adrian–Crump–Moench (ACM) or Kim–Wright. Different models can disagree by 20 bps or more, so a single point estimate should be taken as a guide, not a fact.
