---
term: Yield curve
shortDef: A line plotting Treasury yields across maturities. Its shape — upward, flat, or inverted — is one of the most-watched signals in finance.
category: rates
---

The Treasury yield curve plots the yield-to-maturity of US Treasury securities against their time to maturity. The "normal" shape is upward-sloping: investors demand more yield to lock up money for longer, both because of inflation risk and because rates could rise.

When the curve **inverts** (short yields higher than long yields), it usually means the market expects the Fed to cut rates in the future — which itself usually means a recession is coming. Inversion has preceded every US recession since 1969, though the lead time varies from 6 to 24 months.
