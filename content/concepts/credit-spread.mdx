---
term: Credit spread
shortDef: The extra yield a corporate bond pays over a Treasury of the same maturity. Quoted as an option-adjusted spread (OAS) in basis points. Tight spreads mean investors are paid little to take credit risk.
category: credit
---

Investment-grade (IG) OAS typically sits between 90 and 150 bps; high-yield (HY) OAS typically sits between 350 and 600. Below those ranges is a "carry-positive complacency" regime — investors are paid almost nothing extra to lend to weaker borrowers, and small surprises tend to widen spreads quickly because there is no cushion.

Spreads are the credit market's version of the VIX. They move slowly when nothing is wrong and discontinuously when something is. A useful rule of thumb: HY OAS doubles from its trough inside 12 months in roughly every cycle on record since 1990.
