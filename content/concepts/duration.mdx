---
term: Duration
shortDef: A measure of how much a bond's price changes when interest rates move. Longer-maturity bonds have higher duration and are more rate-sensitive.
category: rates
---

Duration is the weighted average time until a bond's cash flows are received, expressed in years. It's used as a sensitivity measure: a bond with a duration of 7 years will fall roughly 7% in price if rates rise by 1 percentage point, and rise roughly 7% if rates fall by 1 point.

Two things to remember: (1) duration approximates only small rate moves — for big moves you need *convexity* too; (2) zero-coupon bonds have duration equal to their maturity, but coupon-paying bonds have duration *less* than their maturity, because some cash flows arrive earlier.
