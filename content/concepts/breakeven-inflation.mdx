---
term: Breakeven inflation
shortDef: The market's implied forecast of average inflation over a given horizon, calculated as the spread between a nominal Treasury yield and an inflation-protected (TIPS) yield of the same maturity.
category: rates
---

A 10-year nominal Treasury yields some rate (call it 4.30%); a 10-year TIPS yields a real rate (call it 1.85%). The 10-year breakeven is the difference: 2.45%. That's the rate of inflation the bond market is pricing — if realised inflation matches the breakeven, an investor is indifferent between holding nominal and TIPS.

Breakevens are noisy with liquidity premiums (TIPS are less liquid than nominals, especially long-dated) but they're the cleanest market-implied inflation signal we have. Forward breakevens — like 5y5y forward, the implied 5-year breakeven starting 5 years from today — strip out near-term shocks and isolate long-run expectations. Watching forward breakevens is how you tell a transient supply shock from a re-anchoring of expectations.
