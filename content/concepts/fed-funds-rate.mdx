---
term: Fed funds rate
shortDef: The interest rate at which US banks lend reserves to each other overnight. It's the Fed's primary lever for setting the price of money in the economy.
category: monetary-policy
---

The federal funds rate is the interest rate at which depository institutions lend reserve balances to other depository institutions overnight, on an uncollateralized basis. The Fed targets this rate (technically a *range*, currently set in 25-basis-point increments) and uses open market operations and interest on reserves to keep the effective rate close to the target.

Why it matters: almost every other interest rate in the US economy is priced as a spread off the fed funds rate, including the prime rate banks charge their best customers, mortgage rates, and corporate borrowing costs. When the Fed moves, it's moving the *anchor* the entire yield curve sits on.
