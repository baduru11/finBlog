The Federal Reserve held the policy rate steady at its May meeting. The market had largely priced this in — fed funds futures gave the hold a 92% probability the morning of the decision. So why did Treasuries still rally on the press conference, and why did the dollar weaken against the euro by half a percent in twenty minutes?

Because the *path* changed, not the *level*.

## The pause is about the second half

Powell's prepared remarks were carefully written. He repeated the phrase "patient" three times in eight minutes — not a coincidence. The committee is signalling that even if April CPI surprises to the upside, they are not in a rush. The reason is straightforward: services ex-housing, the so-called "supercore" measure, is still running above target on a three-month annualised basis, but goods disinflation has resumed after a brief Q1 stall.

<Callout variant="insight" label="Takeaway">
  The pause is the Fed buying optionality. They want to see at least two more
  CPI prints before deciding whether the next move is a cut or a hold.
</Callout>

## What the dot plot said that the statement didn't

The updated [Summary of Economic Projections](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomc.htm) was the real message. The median dot for end-2026 dropped 25 bps versus March, to 3.875%. Crucially, the *dispersion* widened: three participants now see no cuts this year, while four see two or more. That kind of spread is unusual outside of inflection points.

For comparison, the [March 19 SEP](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/fomcprojtabl20260319.pdf) had a tighter cluster — just one outlier participant on either side of the median. Six weeks of data has visibly shifted opinion on the committee. Whether that's because of the soft April retail-sales print (-0.4%), the unexpectedly weak Q1 GDP advance estimate (1.6% annualised), or both, the dots show real disagreement now.

<ConceptBox term="Dot plot dispersion">
  Each FOMC participant submits an anonymous projection for the policy rate.
  When the spread between the highest and lowest dot widens, it signals real
  disagreement on the committee — usually a sign that the data is genuinely
  ambiguous, not that any one participant is being eccentric.
</ConceptBox>

## The bond market's interpretation

The 2-year yield fell 7 bps to 4.74%; the 10-year fell 9 bps to 4.31%. That's a small bull steepener, but the more interesting move was in 5y5y forward inflation breakevens, which dropped 4 bps to 2.18%. In other words: the market read the pause as *credible* — it doesn't require the Fed to over-tighten to bring inflation back to target.

That's a meaningful endorsement. If markets thought the pause was a policy error, breakevens would have *risen* on the news.

## What I'm watching next

1. **April CPI (May 13).** If supercore prints below 0.30% month-on-month, July becomes a live meeting. Consensus is 0.32%; the [BLS release calendar](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm) lists 8:30 ET as usual.
2. **Q1 GDP revisions (May 28).** The advance estimate showed 1.6% annualised; if the second estimate revises down to 1.3% or lower, the soft-landing narrative gets stronger and the cutting path gets clearer.
3. **Fed-speak.** Watch the regional bank presidents who voted to hold but were known to lean dovish in March. If they start talking about cuts publicly between now and June 11, the next meeting moves. The [public Fed-speak schedule](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/calendar.htm) is the cleanest place to track who's talking when.

None of this changes the big picture: the Fed thinks it can stick the landing. May was just another month of running out the clock.
