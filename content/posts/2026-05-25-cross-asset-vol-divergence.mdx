Eight straight up weeks for the S&P 500. The 30-year Treasury at 5.046%. The VIX at 16.70. The MOVE index at 78.43. Investment-grade credit at roughly 80 bps over Treasuries, high yield at 286 bps. All of those are May 22, 2026 closing prints. Three of them say risk is asleep. One says risk just had its biggest repricing in 19 years.

The most expensive part of this rally is not the index level. It is the assumption sitting under every one of those marks: that 5% long bonds and a sleeping vol surface is the new steady state, not a transition.

## What each market is actually saying

**Long Treasuries are repriced.** The [May 13 30-year auction](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0489) cleared at a 5.046% high yield — the first 30-year to print with a 5% handle since August 2007. The bid-to-cover was 2.303, the weakest since November 2025. That is real risk premium being added — supply, fiscal, and political-Fed risk that the May 22 White House oath did not retire.

<ConceptBox term="MOVE index">Bond options pricing — a low MOVE alongside a high 30-year yield means the option market is treating that yield as permanent, not in motion.</ConceptBox>

**Equity vol is gone.** The VIX at 16.70 sits in the bottom quartile of its history. Realized one-month S&P vol is in the single digits. The S&P 500 closed May 22 at 7,473.47, the eighth consecutive up week — the longest streak since December 2023. The forward 12-month P/E is 22.5x, about 14% above the 10-year average of 18.9x.

**Credit is tight.** The [ICE BofA US High Yield OAS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLH0A0HYM2) is 286 bps, inside its 25-year median of roughly 470. Investment grade is near 80, BBB near 100, AA near 50. Spreads tighter than this since 1997 happened twice — in 2007 and 2021 — and both troughs were followed by HY OAS more than doubling inside 12 months.

**Bond vol is also gone.** MOVE at 78.43 is well below its post-2022 average of about 110. So Treasury option dealers are pricing the same "stable equilibrium" view the equity-option desk is.

## The trade these four prices imply

Stack them and the implied worldview is consistent and tight: long-end rates have finished moving up, they will stay there, growth will not break, earnings will compound at the 21% rate currently sitting in 2026 consensus, and no leveraged corner of the system breaks along the way.

That is a coherent thesis. It is also a leveraged bet — leveraged in the sense that *every market* has to keep being right at the same time. There is no embedded protection in the price of any of the four against the other three being wrong.

<Callout variant="insight" label="Takeaway">
Equity vol, bond vol, and credit spreads are all priced as if the 30-year at 5% is the destination. If it is a waystation, the three reprice together — not separately.
</Callout>

## When the four have aligned like this before

Two reference points, with the usual caveat that the analog is structural, not predictive.

January 2018: VIX near 11, IG OAS in the 80s, HY OAS in the low 300s, 10-year yields rising into a Fed hiking cycle. In early February the VIX took out 50 in a single session, the short-vol complex blew up, and HY spreads widened more than 100 bps before year-end.

Mid-2007: HY OAS troughed near 240 bps in May, with the 10-year above 4.9% and VIX near 13. By August HY had doubled to 466. The level signal (rising long yields) had been visible for months before the vol surface noticed.

The 2026 setup is not 2007 — bank balance sheets and money-market plumbing are stronger, and the household sector is less levered. But the *cross-asset shape* of the trade is the same: every options market and every spread is pricing the same view that the new normal is calm, and the long-end yield is the only thing that has been allowed to move.

## What I'm watching

- **MOVE above 90.** A sustained break of the post-2022 floor would be the cleanest signal that bond-option dealers are starting to charge for tail risk again. Today: 78.43.
- **HY OAS above 350.** A 60 bps widening from here, on no specific catalyst, is the credit-market version of the same signal. Today: 286 bps.
- **The 30-year clearing above 5.20%.** The May 13 stop was 5.046%. A clean break of 5.20% at the [next quarterly refunding (August 2026)](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financing-the-government/quarterly-refunding) puts the term-premium repricing story back on the front page.
- **The S&P 500's rolling correlation with the 30-year yield.** It has been positive since mid-April — stocks and long bonds both up. A flip back to negative is the regime change.
- **The June FOMC SEP, released June 17, 2026.** First [Summary of Economic Projections](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm) under Chair Warsh. If the long-run rate dot moves up from 3.0% to 3.5%, every one of the four prices above has a fundamentals reason to reprice — at the same time.
